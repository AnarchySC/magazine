Everyone knows the price ceiling protects you. That's the premise Earth's Energy Oversight Directorate has sold for twenty generations: when wholesale gas spikes, the ceiling holds, and households are spared the worst of it.

Ask yourself what a ceiling is for, though, if it rises every time someone points a weapon at a gas field.

This season the Directorate's own commissioned forecast, drafted by Helios Grid Consortium, projects the ceiling climbing 27 percent by January — to roughly 4,100 SGC a year for an average household, a new record, surpassing the previous record set three years prior during an earlier flare-up in the same disputed thermal corridor. Wholesale gas across the Core Systems hit a four-year high this week, pushed there by renewed strikes between Earth factions contesting extraction rights along the Perseid Corridor — a fight over hydrocarbon fields that, from where I sit, looks exactly like the fight that preceded it, and the one before that.

I did not come here to adjudicate the Corridor. I came here to tell you what the rest of the galaxy already concluded about it.

The Kel'vari Trade Consortium — the body that first credentialed me, and the body that quietly rates every inhabited world as a long-term energy counterparty — downgraded Earth's reliability index twice in the last standard decade. Not for scarcity. Earth has no shortage of sun, tide, or geothermal flux. It was downgraded for correlation risk: the tendency of its domestic quarrels to show up, with perfect reliability, in the heating bill of someone six systems away who never asked to fund either side.

This is not a new pattern. The Drevnik Compact ran its home systems on combustible gas harvested from a contested moon for nearly four centuries, insisting at every council session that the price ceiling was a consumer protection and not, as their own treasury records eventually showed, a mechanism for converting conflict into quarterly revenue for whoever held the extraction license. They did not fix this by regulating the ceiling harder. They fixed it by making the fuel irrelevant — fusion-grid integration, decades late, resisted the whole way by the same ministries that had built careers managing the 'crisis.'

Earth has had access to the same fusion-grid architecture for over a century, through trade terms the Consortium has offered three times and the Earth Unified Council has declined three times, citing 'energy sovereignty.' What does that sovereignty actually buy you? This winter, the answer is 11.13 units per kilowatt-hour equivalent — a record.

Nobody is forcing the Corridor's combatants to keep fighting over a fuel the rest of the galaxy stopped needing a thousand years ago. Nobody is forcing the Directorate to keep calling a pass-through mechanism a 'cap.' And nobody, certainly not the Consortium, is forcing Earth to remain the one inhabited world whose heating bill functions as a leading indicator of whose pipeline got hit this month.

The ceiling was never going to protect you. It was only ever going to tell you, eventually, how much the fight cost.
