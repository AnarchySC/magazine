The transmission ran ninety minutes, free to anyone with a receiver tuned to the Uwelo Relay out of Jarnava Drift. No subscription wall. No sponsor jingle up front. Just a man named Marlow Robik explaining, in the unhurried cadence of someone who has said this a thousand times, why every colonist's exhaustion traces back to a single missing trace element: copper.

Robik calls it the Core Mineral Protocol. The pitch is simple. GCB-subsidized ration bricks, the ones stacked floor to ceiling in every Colony Administration depot, strip copper out during processing to extend shelf life across six-month freight runs. Strip the copper, starve the mitochondria, and you get a settlement full of people too tired to question anything — including, Robik notes, monetary policy. It is a tidy theory. Tidy theories move product.

The module set costs 1,200 SGC, paid in four installments, and graduates are required — not encouraged, required — to source their supplementation exclusively through Certified Copper Tincture, the only preparation Robik's literature recognizes as "bioavailable." The tincture is refined from ore out of a single claim: the Pellyn Rock concession, registered three years ago to a holding company called Griffon Extraction Guild.

Griffon Extraction Guild is named for Edric Griffon, the pamphleteer who built his reputation on forty years of warning frontier audiences away from the Galactic Central Bank, and who happened to host the broadcast that introduced Robik to half a million listeners. Griffon does not sell the tincture himself. He does not need to. He sells the ore to the three licensed refiners who do, at 40 SGC per kilogram, a price that undercuts the independent assay guilds on Kessa Station by just enough to make switching suppliers irrational.

No cargo manifest, no bill of lading, carries a line item for ideology. It carries weight, origin, and consignee. Run the numbers on Pellyn Rock's export logs and the volume leaving that moon over the past eighteen months tracks almost exactly with Protocol enrollment figures, delayed by the six weeks a freight run takes to clear customs at Jarnava. That is not a coincidence built on belief. That is a coincidence built on a loading schedule.

The Protocol's marketing never mentions the concession. It mentions freedom from institutional capture, the tyranny of processed rations, the courage to question the official nutrition consensus. All of which can be true and still sit beside an invoice. A man can sincerely believe copper deficiency is killing the frontier and still own the only mine selling the cure. History has not recorded many prophets who turned down the toll booth once they'd built the road.

Somewhere between the broadcast and the depot, a listener paid 1,200 SGC to be told the system was lying to him, then paid again, in tincture, to a man who owns the ore. Who audits the auditor of distrust?
