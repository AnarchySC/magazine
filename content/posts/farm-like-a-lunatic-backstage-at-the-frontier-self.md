The fastest-growing subculture in the Outer Rim right now isn't a sound, a fashion line, or a holovid franchise. It's dirt.

For the past two cycles, Core Systems dropouts — the ones priced out of the vertical cities or just tired of recycled air — have been migrating to terraformed frontier moons and doing something radical: farming. Not corp-contract farming. Lunatic farming. That's literally the branding, courtesy of a weathered frontier legend named Joel Saltrin, whose teachings have quietly become the Outer Rim's answer to a music movement — complete with a guru, a mythology, and now a masterclass with 212 video modules and 49 hours of runtime, circulating the system-net like the new underground tape trade.

The diagram, if you need one: at the center is Saltrin, decades deep on Polyface Moon, preaching six commandments that have become gospel among the hab-sled crowd.

- **Dig ponds first.** Not for aesthetics — for water reclamation and access. Terraformed soil without catchment is just expensive sand.
- **Ponds create access.** Roads, irrigation, firebreaks — one hole in the ground, six functions. The frontier equivalent of a riff that also fixes your amp.
- **"Can-do" over credentials.** Nobody out there cares about your Core Systems agri-license. They care if you can fix a pump at 0300 with a torch and a prayer.
- **Relationships are the real equity.** Saltrin calls it a "guild of mastery" — a trust network of settlers who'll lend you a part, a hand, or a warning before the Colony Administration even shows up to inspect anything.
- **Location is strategic, not romantic.** Proximity to a market station beats a pretty view every time. Nobody's buying your gravity-bred poultry if it's four burns away.
- **Mobile infrastructure is everything.** Hab-sleds and modular pens that move mean sunlight and fallow cycles do your disease control for you. No sterile domes, no pharma contracts — just rotate and rest.

The part that gets me buzzing: this isn't being marketed as agriculture. It's being marketed as identity. The aesthetic — sun-bleached coveralls, hand-welded hab-sleds, that specific squint of someone who hasn't seen a neural feed in a week — is showing up in fashion spreads from Titan to the Kardashia Corridor. Kids who'll never touch a plow are buying the look.

And there's a manufactured layer underneath it, too: the free eBook funnel, the membership tiers, the "join for exclusive perks" pitch — that's scene-building 101, same playbook as any underground label turning a sound into a product line. But beneath the funnel is something real: actual families on actual moons, building actual water systems with their hands, opting out of a system that increasingly asks permission for you to exist.

That tension — genuine self-reliance wrapped in a subscription model — might be the most 2935 thing I've covered all cycle.
