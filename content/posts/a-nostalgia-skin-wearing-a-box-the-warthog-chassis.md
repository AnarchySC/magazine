Let's see if this works.

That's what I said when the Warthog arrived at the public lab three weeks late - Ingot Dynamics blamed a packing-foam gap, then blamed the gap-fix, then just shipped it anyway. Fine. Delays are forgivable. What isn't forgivable is charging 180 to 200 SGC for a chassis that is, underneath the olive-drab paint and stenciled rivets, the exact same Lattice-4000D open frame we reviewed favorably last cycle for less than half that.

The Warthog is styled after an extinct Earth atmospheric attack craft from some ancient flatscreen combat simulation - we're told the silhouette predates the Interstellar Assembly entirely. Switch guards, a flip-up I/O cover, a fabric tag that says something like 'remove before launch.' None of it does anything. It's just cool, and that's allowed to be enough sometimes. I have no complaint about joy.

My complaint is about the invoice.

**What we actually measured**

Same cognition-core clearance (400mm with front fans in), same seven horizontal slots, same pegboard-style core tray that lets you reposition the cable ties anywhere on the grid instead of fighting fixed loop points. That pegboard is the one genuinely good idea in here, and it isn't new - it's geometry, not magic. Distribute the anchor points evenly and the cable routing stops fighting your hands. Ingot didn't invent that. They licensed it from the Lattice frame and painted it green.

Thermals on the RS variant (two 200mm intakes, one 120mm exhaust, pre-installed) ran within margin-of-error of the bare 4000D under identical processing-core load. Noise floor, same. The side panels snap rather than bolt, which saves nothing functionally but explains the shipping gap perfectly - nothing's holding them shut but friction and hope.

**The part I actually care about**

Ingot posted printable accessory files to the open model archive - a triple-drive cage, a top dust-filter frame - and to their credit, the instructions are detailed and the geometry is sound. I printed the drive cage myself: one full day on the lab's slowest extruder, a few extra bolts not included in the kit, but it works, and it's free if you own a printer and twelve hours of patience. That's the right-to-fabricate model working as it should.

What doesn't work: the shroud files from the plain 4000D don't fit the Warthog, because Ingot changed the side-panel ventilation just enough to break compatibility, cosmetic choice rippling into functional lock-in. Nothing stops them from noting that in the file description. They just didn't.

So: buy it if the paint speaks to you, not because anything inside performs differently. And if you want the pegboard trick without the markup, the Lattice-4000D schematic is sitting in the archive, unlocked, for half the credits. I've posted our full thermal log and the drive-cage print settings alongside it. Build it yourself, and tell us how it runs.
