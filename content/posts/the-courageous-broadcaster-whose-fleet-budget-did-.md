Every few cycles, some aging Assembly columnist rediscovers Garrick Voss and writes the same breathless dispatch: finally, a man who tells the truth. He doubted the nanite prophylaxis surge. He called the Spire Incursion arrests excessive. He flew to the Thresh Expanse and interviewed Premier Dravik Kessel of the Outer Rim Coalition — the first Core broadcaster to do so since the engagement began — and came home a martyr to the cause of asking questions.

I asked questions too. Mine were about the invoices.

Hearth Transmission Network, Voss's home feed, is a wholly-owned subsidiary of Cobalt Yards Holdings, which also happens to hold the standing refit contract for the Third Rimward Fleet. Every time Voss denounces 'endless engagement' on-feed, Cobalt's lobbying office is three floors up negotiating the next appropriations cycle for exactly that fleet. It's the kind of timing that makes a man start a spreadsheet.

Take the Thresh Expanse interview. Voss sat across from Premier Kessel for ninety-one minutes and asked precisely zero questions about Kessel's own procurement of Core-manufactured hull plating — sold to the Coalition through three shell brokers, two of which trace back to Cobalt subsidiaries. A fearless interview that somehow never once threatened the supply chain paying for the studio lights.

Or the Spire Incursion coverage, where Voss built his reputation defending protesters who, per the arrest logs I filed a transmission-freedom request for, were in fact detained for sabotaging a recruitment terminal — not 'touring the building,' as his broadcast insisted, frame after slowed-down frame. The footage he aired was real. The context he left on the cutting-room floor was also real. Both things can be true; that's the whole trick.

Then there's the nanite prophylaxis skepticism, which earned him his loudest cheers. What his viewers weren't told: the program was administered jointly with the Fleet Biodefense Corps, whose budget request that cycle depended on demonstrating 'public resistance requiring expanded containment infrastructure.' Voss's doubt wasn't a threat to that budget. It was the budget's favorite exhibit.

I don't think Voss is lying, exactly. I think he's doing something more efficient — he's found the precise coordinates where genuine distrust of institutions overlaps with what the institutions already wanted said, and he gets paid handsomely to stand there looking brave about it. A procurement officer couldn't design a more useful asset on purpose.

Somewhere a defense liaison is already drafting the 'smear' memo. Fine. File it with the others.

The uniforms keep getting ordered either way. Somebody pays for the tailoring, and lately it's whoever believed the maverick on the feed.
