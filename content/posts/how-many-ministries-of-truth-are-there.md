I went to Albion Drift to write about patrol-drone response times. I left with an org chart nobody outside the Proconsul's Office is supposed to see, and a new appreciation for how much bureaucracy you can stack on top of a single citizen's mobi-feed before anyone notices it's a cage.

Start with Joen Mye. Two patrol units showed up at her hab-pod last cycle to deliver an official caution — for a neural-feed post she never made. Someone else's words, her account tagged by a matching algorithm nobody will name. The caution stands anyway. Nobody's filed a retraction.

Or take Grahm Linnehan, the writer behind two of the funniest serials ever broadcast on the Core relay. Detained at a transit dock over jokes he told half a standard year earlier. Eventually the Directorate apologized and cut him 25,000 SGC in compensation — which tells you the arrest was always indefensible, just not expensive enough to stop them trying.

According to the station's own watchdog report, Albion Drift has logged 62,199 arrests for "communications offenses" over five cycles. More than the entire Outer Rim Coalition combined, which is the bloc Albion's Proconsul loves to call the civilization that doesn't understand free expression.

The report doesn't map one thing: how many separate bureaus it takes to generate that number.

I posed as a compliance auditor — a persona built over three weeks with a contact who owed me from a currency job I won't detail here — and got two hours inside the newest addition, the National Cognition Integrity Directorate, announced by Proconsul Burnholt at the last Interstellar Assembly session as a shield against "hostile-bloc disinformation and fabricated transmissions damaging social cohesion." Translation, straight from a staffer who thought I had clearance: flag whatever the Proconsul doesn't like, attribute it to the Rim, route it to deletion.

NCID doesn't operate alone. It sits above the Record Integrity & Cultural Unit, which maintains an internal list of "key texts of interest to extremist audiences." I photographed the list. It includes Tolkien's Ring Cycle, Huxley, the collected Old Earth poets, a historical document called Nineteen Eighty-Four that apparently nobody in the Directorate found ironic, and — I am not inventing this — a century-old travel documentary about rail journeys through the Core. Interest in trains is now a warning sign.

Below that sits the Narrative Stability team, rebranded three times since its founding as a pandemic-era messaging cell, whose current job is "understanding disinformation narratives" by manufacturing counter-narratives and measuring how far they spread. Fourteen units total, by my contact's count, nested across two offices, none listed on any public organizational page.

I transmitted this file from orbit, already burning fuel toward the next jump. If the Directorate wants to flag this dispatch as hostile disinformation, they're welcome to add it to the list. I hear Tolkien's in good company.
