**Okay, here's the thing nobody's telling you —** the most electrifying piece of horror fiction burning through Neural feeds right now isn't coming from a mega-corp studio with a three-trillion SGC production budget. It's a short-form serial called ***Void Reckoning***, it's running in eighteen-minute episodes, and episode three just made half the galaxy put down their food.

Let me show you how this actually works.

## The Setup

The premise sounds simple. Too simple. A licensed *Sanctioned Termination Specialist* — that's the official 2935 certification for what we used to just call a hunter-for-hire — is drifting through a decommissioned ore corridor somewhere in the asteroid scatter between Ceres and the Jovian settlements. She's starving. Her navigation rig is dead. She's looking for her partner, who went silent three rotations ago.

Then: a single gunshot sound through the bulkhead. Then: something that is *not* her partner.

The creature — the production team is calling it a *fissure stalker*, and I need you to understand they designed this thing with actual xenobiological consultants — is everything the void has ever threatened to be. It's wrong in the way that deep-space fauna gets wrong when it's been evolving in the dark with zero competition for about four million years. It is very good at being alive and very indifferent to whether you are.

## AND HERE'S WHERE IT GETS INTERESTING.

The confrontation at the corridor's end isn't what you expect. It's not a chase. It's not a fight sequence. It's a *standoff* — and when the Specialist finally has the creature cornered under a flickering mag-lamp, she doesn't pull the trigger.

She talks.

And in that conversation — or whatever you call it when one party is a licensed killer and the other party is a three-metric-ton apex predator made of regolith-adapted cartilage — she says the line that broke Neural feeds galaxy-wide: *"I am a killer. You are a killer. We are the only honest things in this corridor."*

The confession isn't about the creature.

## Why This Is the Real Story

Forget what you heard about this being a horror piece. Yes, it's horrifying. Yes, episode two has a sequence that reportedly caused seventeen documented cases of mobi-device-throwing on Titan Station alone. But *Void Reckoning* is doing something sneaky under the blood and the darkness.

It's asking who society actually certifies as dangerous — and why.

The Specialist holds a legal document. She has a stamp from the Colony Administration. She has a rate card, a client portfolio, a kill log filed quarterly with the Bureau of Sanctioned Operations. The creature has none of these things. The creature is simply *what it is.*

You're gonna want to remember this name: **Sora Vance**, the writer-director behind this. Former forensic psychology student turned experimental film-maker out of the Callisto underground circuit. She's been making short horror pieces for six years that maybe two thousand people have seen. *Void Reckoning* just crossed forty-two million Neural feed views in nine days.

## The Confession Problem

What the galaxy is actually arguing about — and the forums are *magnificent* right now, genuinely chaotic — is whether the Specialist's confession makes her more human or less. Whether naming yourself a killer is honesty or absolution. Whether the creature's "monstrousness" was ever really the point.

The creature doesn't get a certification. It doesn't need one. It just *is.*

The Specialist spent twelve years earning paperwork that says she's allowed to be what she naturally is.

Somebody tell me which one of them is the monster. Take your time. I'll wait.

*Void Reckoning* episodes one through three are available now on independent transmission channels. Episode four drops next cycle. Do not watch episode two alone.
