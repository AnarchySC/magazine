Okay okay okay, let me draw this out for you—

Three nights ago, the overnight funding rate on the Ceres Exchange repo desk — where mega-corps borrow cargo-backed collateral against each other for twelve hours at a stretch — jumped from 2.1% to 6.4%. In four hours. Then the Galactic Central Bank quietly wired eighteen trillion SGC into the overnight window before the morning bell, and by breakfast everyone was pretending it never happened.

Picture the repo market as the plumbing behind your station's walls. Nobody watches plumbing. Nobody wants to. But when the pipes scream at 3am, you learn fast that every fancy fixture in the penthouse — every zero-coupon terraforming bond, every leveraged hab-ring derivative, every 'guaranteed yield' annuity sold to colonists on Pallas Drift — was resting on the same cheap, overnight, roll-it-every-day credit.

The bubble never announces itself at the top. It announces itself in the basement, where the collateral lives.

Why did the pipes scream? Because mega-corps — Stellar Financial, Orion Trust, the usual cast — have spent three years parking reserves in terraforming bonds paying 9%, funded almost entirely with overnight borrowing at 2%. Free money, right up until the night somebody needed actual cash instead of a promise, and discovered there wasn't enough real liquidity in the system to cover it. So the rate for borrowing cash for half a day tripled, because suddenly everyone wanted cash and nobody had any to lend.

This is the Cantillon Effect wearing a work uniform. The GCB's eighteen trillion didn't go to colonists. It went straight into the institutions that built the leverage tower in the first place, refilling the same pipes that just failed, before the retail investor even heard the word 'repo.' By the time credit dilution reaches a grocery bill on the frontier settlements, the mega-corps have already been bailed out twice.

And this wasn't the first midnight spike — it's the third this cycle, each one bigger, each one patched faster and quieter than the last. That's not stability. That's a system that needs resuscitation every few weeks and calls it 'liquidity management.'

Sound money doesn't need a repo desk flashing red at 3am. It doesn't need eighteen trillion credits materialized from nothing to keep a bond market breathing. When the plumbing has to scream before anyone fixes it, you're not looking at a resilient system — you're looking at an expensive, leveraged bet that the next scream doesn't come from a pipe the GCB can't reach in time.

Watch the overnight rate. Not the headlines. The headlines will tell you everything's fine right up until it isn't.
