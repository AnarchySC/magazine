Thirty-four standard years ago, two towers on the Earth Unified Council's home station came down in under ninety minutes. A third tower, Spire Seven, fell seven hours later without having been struck by anything at all. The official record calls this a coincidence of structural fatigue. A growing number of people under thirty call it something else.

The Interstellar Opinion Bureau polled 1,098 Core Systems adults this cycle. Forty-one percent of Driftborn respondents — the generation born in the attack's shadow — said some officials within the Council knew the strike was coming and let it proceed. Among the next bracket up, that number climbs to 46%. Among the Founders generation, the ones who actually lived through it, it drops to 30%.

Sit with that inversion. The people who were there trust the story more than the people who weren't. That is not what institutional credibility is supposed to look like. That is what institutional credibility looks like when it's running on fumes.

Twenty-seven percent of Driftborn go further and say the Council was behind the attack outright — orchestrated it, not merely permitted it. Nine percent of Founders agree. On Spire Seven specifically, 36% of the youngest respondents believe it was brought down by deliberate charge deployment. I asked a Council archivist last month whether Spire Seven's collapse had ever been independently modeled by anyone outside Council engineering. He told me the data was 'under continued review.' It has been under continued review for thirty-four years. I don't deal in intentions. I deal in actions, and the action here is three decades of refusing to release a structural report.

The generations split the other way on foreign involvement. A plurality across every age bracket believes Outer Rim Coalition officials had a hand in the planning — Founders at 47%, Driftborn at 37%. Suspicion of the Coalition is evidently permitted to age well. Suspicion of the Council is not.

The Helion Syndicate question splits hardest of all: 35% of Driftborn consider Syndicate involvement plausible, against just 5% of Founders. Two attacks, two generational memories, and the Council has never had to answer for either version, because it has spent thirty-four years treating the question itself as the scandal.

This isn't a new pattern. Bureau archives show skepticism climbing in nearly every survey since the attack — 26% said 'let it happen' within three years of the collapse, rising past 40% within two decades. A Council press officer told the Earth Network News desk this week that 'dwelling on these events serves no forward purpose.' I'd have asked her to define forward purpose, but she'd already left the room. That's the Council's actual position, stated plainly for once: the dead don't need an answer, and the living are advised to stop asking for one.

They will keep asking. The numbers say so.
