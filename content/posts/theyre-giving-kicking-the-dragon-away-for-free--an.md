Back when I hauled ice out past the Vesta lanes, there was a dock supervisor at Transfer Node Six named Brakk who charged what he called a "flow fee." You'd already paid for your slip. You'd already cleared cargo inspection. But if you wanted your barge moved through the queue before next shift change, you slipped Brakk a little something extra. Nobody told you to. Nobody wrote it down anywhere. You just did it, because everybody did it, because the one time a kid named Oresh didn't do it, his barge sat in the queue for eleven hours and everybody decided that was the dragon's teeth showing.

Took me two years to notice Brakk never actually moved anybody faster. The queue ran on its own clock. We were all just paying a toll to a fear we'd built ourselves and handed him the collection rights.

I bring this up because a broadcaster out of the Kardashia drift stations, a fellow who goes by Corwin Vesk on the independent transmissions, just dropped the full audio cut of his work *Kicking the Dragon* across the system-net. Every relay, every colony feed, Core Systems to the Rim. Cost to you: zero. No subscription gate, no credit-chain lock, no "premium tier unlocks chapter nine." You listen or you don't.

At the bottom of the transmission there's a list. If the thing was worth something to you, here's where you can send value — a half-dozen different credit-chains, Lumen and Ferrite and Cinder and a few others I won't bore you listing, plus a physical courier address on some backwater station for anyone who'd rather mail an actual data-cassette by hand because they don't trust the chains at all.

Nobody's garnishing anything. No enforcement drone shows up if you skip the list entirely. No fee gets auto-deducted from your mobi account three years from now because you forgot to read a renewal clause. You get the work. You decide, with your own judgment, what it was worth to you, and you send that amount through whichever rail you personally trust, or you send nothing, and either way Vesk doesn't send anyone after you.

That's not charity. That's not a business model, exactly, either. That's just what trade looks like with the fear surgically removed from it — the thing Brakk's flow fee never was, the thing half of what gets billed to you by Colony Administrations never is.

The real question is why we've gotten so used to paying for things we never agreed to that a man offering something for free, asking for nothing, strikes us as the strange one.

Nobody's going to kick your particular dragon for you. But it might be worth noticing how many of the fees you pay are bills from a queue that was never actually timed, collected by a supervisor who never actually controlled the clock.
