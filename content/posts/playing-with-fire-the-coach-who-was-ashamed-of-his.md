Before tip-off, they strapped magnetic championship rings onto grown men's fingers in front of sixteen thousand fans who paid station-docking prices to watch it happen. Jael Brunsk, point guard and designated balm for every Voidhawks crisis, thanked the crowd at Madison Orbital Garden for not drifting out of their harness-seats during last spring's 29-point third-quarter collapse. The product failed so badly that staying seated became the heroic labor worth commemorating on banner night.

Then the hologram unfurled, Walt "Clyde" Fraziir and Earl "The Pearl" Monvroe clipped rings onto rookies, and the widow of franchise legend Willis Reeth placed one on Coach Mika Braun's hand to a standing ovation. Nobody asked what that alloy cost to mine, on which low-grav rock, or whose exoskeleton gave out extracting it. The spectacle doesn't audit its supply chain. It just needs the ovation on tape.

Then they played the actual game and lost, 106-100, to the Philadelphia Rim Reapers. Brunsk shot 6-of-19, missed four of five in the fourth quarter under full centrifugal spin — a detail the broadcast called "nerves" and I'd call a league dialing up habitat rotation during crunch time without disclosing the g-load change to the athletes' own medical staff. Karlo-Anton Tovn picked up early foul trouble, a penalty structure that functions exactly like wage-docking: perform under threat of removal, or sit and forfeit your cut of the night's merch pool.

Braun admitted, in front of a room full of reporters, that if Brunsk's late shots had fallen and the Voidhawks had stolen the win, he "wouldn't have felt good about it." Playing with fire, he called it. No water, no fire blanket — a coach confessing the organization's whole operating model is combustible, written up as a quirky quote instead of a confession.

Brunsk, for his part, said it was good to get "knocked down off the mountain," to humble the ego. Humility is free. The ring ceremony's broadcast rights were not. Someone extracted a tidy margin off 29 points of fan suffering turned into content, off a widow's grief turned into applause, off a rookie's finger turned into a prop — and that someone was not wearing a jersey.

Nobody in that arena asked who sets the spin-rate dial, who owns the merch concession on ring night, or what cut of tonight's gate goes to the athlete injury fund versus the shareholder distribution. They asked whether Brunsk's slump was "mental." It's not mental. It's structural. The scoreboard read 106-100. The real score — margin extracted, labor donated, loyalty monetized — never gets posted anywhere you can see it.

I wrote this on three protein bars and half a bag of pretzel dust. I regret nothing except not saying more of it to Braun's face.
