Provence Station doesn't look like a war zone from the transit ring. It looks like what it is: a Core Systems colony that promised every arriving family a stipend, a berth, and a fabrication voucher, and kept that promise for about forty years longer than its supply chain could handle.

Then the Frontier migration surge hit — forty thousand new residents in eighteen months, fleeing collapsed mining concerns on the Rim — and the dole line stopped reaching the back of the queue. Everyone agrees on that part. Where it gets interesting is what happened next, because what happened next is a business.

ENN's framing is tidy: "student agitators," a "coordinated tactic," five thousand teenagers arrested, six hundred-plus peacekeeping officers injured, seven confirmed hydrochloric acid attacks on enforcement details, a fifteen-year-old charged with burning a woman's face using a jury-rigged flame unit. The Gallic Colony Administration calls it an immigration failure and leaves it there. Both stories end exactly where it's convenient for them to end.

I spent six nights in the lower ring wearing a fabricator-repair badge nobody checked twice, because nobody down there expects a journalist to want the smell. The acid isn't homemade. It's industrial feedstock, the same corrosive used to strip hull coating at three licensed yards on the station, diverted through a maintenance contractor who reports shortages to the Administration and sells the surplus three decks down at a 40% markup over black-market rate. The flame rigs are worse — modified fuel igniters from agricultural pod equipment, sold in kits, with a "consultant" walking buyers through assembly for a flat 200 SGC fee. He wouldn't give me a name. He gave me a price list.

The people doing the actual work told me the real crisis isn't ideology, it's inventory. A station medic running triage out of a converted cargo bay — treating rioters and peacekeepers on the same cot rotation, no questions asked — said she's seen the same burn pattern eleven times this month and the same unregistered fuel signature every time. "Nobody's radicalizing these kids," she said. "Somebody's supplying them. There's a difference, and everybody who matters knows it."

The credentialed press skips past that part, because chasing it means admitting the riots are profitable for people who aren't rioting — contractors skimming corrosive stock, kit-sellers running volume discounts, and an Administration that would rather fund enforcement overtime than audit its own maintenance contracts. The teenagers get the charge sheets. The supply chain gets the margin.

I got out through the old freight lock before the ring-wide curfew hit. My exit was already arranged three days prior — habit, not paranoia. The station's still burning in patches as I file this. The dole line is still short. And the invoices, if anyone ever bothered to pull them, would tell a much less convenient story than "coordinated leftist tactic."
