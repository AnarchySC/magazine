Okay, so somebody dug up an old transmission archive this week and I haven't stopped thinking about it since.

The bulletin is called the *Sound Credit Dispatch*, hand-curated for decades by a reclusive archivist out past the Kuiper relay stations, mostly ignored because it looked like the kind of thing your uncle forwards you at 3am. Except this batch of back issues contains something nobody's bothered to deny: full manifest logs from the 2487 "comet-viewing expedition" to Hollow Reach Station.

Here's the whiteboard moment.

Six passengers boarded a privately chartered, radiation-shielded transport under assumed names. Official cover story: amateur astronomy, watching a comet nobody back home had ever heard of. Real purpose, confirmed by the charter manifest itself: draft the founding charter of what would become the Galactic Central Bank.

Who were the six?

- A senior director from what's now Stellar Financial
- The Earth Treasury's top monetary advisor at the time
- Representatives from two proto-mega-corps that merged into Orion Trust
- A "neutral academic" whose transit fees were, delightfully, paid by the same corps
- One assistant whose job was apparently just to keep everyone's assumed names straight

So the people who designed the institution meant to supervise credit issuance across every colonized system were, at the time, employees of the institutions that stood to benefit most from issuing that credit first.

That's not a conspiracy theory. That's the manifest.

And once you see it, the whole structure stops looking like an accident. The GCB doesn't spray new credits evenly across the galaxy like rain. It injects them at specific docking points — mega-corp balance sheets, sovereign bond desks, favored settlement contractors — and those credits only diffuse outward later, by which point prices on every frontier station have already adjusted. First recipients buy at yesterday's prices. Everyone else pays tomorrow's. That's the Cantillon routing, and it was baked into the architecture by the exact parties sitting closest to the tap.

The Interstellar Assembly's response to the resurfaced logs has been magnificent in its boredom: a spokesperson called it "settled institutional history," which is bureaucrat for *yes, obviously, why are you just now reading the manifest.*

The archivist who republished the bulletin says transmission requests have gone up forty-fold since. Mostly from frontier settlements, where the dilution always lands last and heaviest — the places that get the new credits only after three relay hops and a markup.

Nobody's claiming the comet-hunt cover story was clever. It wasn't. It's just that for nearly five centuries, nobody checked the manifest.

They're checking now.
