Thirty years ago, Corwin Ness was the most bankable director on the Ceres Prime lot. Then a leaked neural-feed recording — a slurred, ugly rant aimed at the Earth Unified Council's Archive Office — ended him. He called them censors. He called them thieves of memory. He was drunk, he was cruel to the technician recording him, and the Guild dropped him within the week. Nobody watched his trilogy the same way again. Most people stopped watching it at all.

I rewatched it this month, because the Archive Office just admitted — quietly, in a filing nobody was meant to read closely — that it spent a decade "harmonizing classification standards" by deleting forty million unlicensed texts from Frontier catalogs. The official statement used the word *stewardship*. Ness used a different word, in 2904, in a film called *The Continuance*. He called it *erasure with a budget line*.

The trilogy's villain, the Continuity Office, doesn't burn books. It reclassifies them — quietly, administratively, one license lapse at a time — until the thing simply isn't anywhere anymore. No raid, no headline, just absence. Critics at the time called this a weak antagonist. Bureaucracy isn't cinematic, they said. Where's the menace? I think they missed that the lack of menace *was* the argument. Ness wasn't warning us about a tyrant. He was warning us that the thing capable of erasing a civilization's memory would look exactly like a filing update, and that we'd let it, because the man saying so was unbearable.

We didn't separate the message from the messenger because the message was wrong. We separated it because Ness made that easy — his own ugliness gave everyone a clean, guilt-free reason to stop listening. The surface story is a disgraced man's films getting a second look. The real story is how comfortably we let someone's personal wreckage do our thinking for us, so we never have to weigh an uncomfortable claim on its own terms.

Now there's a new genre cropping up on the neural-feeds — hour-long "vindication reels," retrospectives insisting Ness saw it coming all along. I watched three of them this week. All three were sponsored by the same aural-implant manufacturer, the kind that cuts in halfway through a sentence to offer you forty percent off a hearing upgrade. There is something almost too on the nose about a redemption arc for a man who warned us about quiet erasure, delivered to you by a company that wants you to pay to hear better.

I don't think Ness was a good man. The recording is still unbearable to listen to. But *The Continuance* never asked to be judged by its maker's character — it asked whether we'd recognize erasure when it came without a villain's face attached. We didn't. We're still not sure we do. That's not a footnote to the scandal. That's the whole review.
