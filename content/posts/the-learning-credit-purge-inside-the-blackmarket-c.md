The classroom had no windows and no transponder signature. Just a dozen kids on folding stools, a salvaged holoscreen flickering between lesson modules, and a woman named Dessa who used to teach licensed curriculum on Kesem Station before she got tired of filing compliance reports to an Instructor Guild that answered to nobody she'd ever met.

This is a Cognition Grant Guild node, unregistered, three decks below a hydroponics bay on a settlement I won't name because the people running it asked me not to, and because I'd like to visit again without an escort.

The mechanics are simple. The Earth Unified Council's last session passed the Unified Prosperity Act, which included a modest provision: the Cognition Freedom Credit, up to 1,700 SGC per adult, 3,400 for a bonded pair, for contributions to Learning-Grant Trusts — private outfits that fund scholarships, including for off-grid home instruction. Thirty-two Colony Administrations signed on. Families used Cognition Savings Ledgers to cover tutors, fabrication kits, neural-net access fees. Parents paying for their own kids' education with their own credits, minus a sliver of tax.

Now the Assembly's incoming majority, swept in on promises to claw back 'runaway concessions' from the last session, is eyeing repeal. The Instructor Guilds, who never liked a credit that let families route around licensed schooling, are lobbying hard. Nobody in the chamber seems to clock that half the new Assembly members got elected in districts that flipped specifically because off-grid parents turned out to vote. Punish them now and you're biting the hand that handed you the seat.

What the floor debate keeps missing: I didn't find one family on this station waiting to see how the vote goes. They built the alternative years ago. No GCB-traceable ledger, no Guild inspector, no credit to lose because they never filed for one in the first place. Dessa's node runs on barter — hydroponics labor, repair work, a cut of whatever the kids build once they're old enough to sell it. Take the credit away and you don't get more licensed classrooms. You get more unlit decks like this one.

There's a wrinkle worth sitting with, though. The loudest voice broadcasting against the repeal, a frontier commentator peddling his own 'Liberty Cognition Curriculum' across six colony feeds, charges more for his courseware than the credit was ever worth. Same racket, different flag. He needs the panic to keep selling. The Assembly needs the panic to keep legislating. Dessa, three decks down, needs neither. She just needs the lights to stay on and the inspectors to stay lost.

That's the whole fight, really — everyone arguing over who gets to subsidize or tax the thing that was already quietly solved by people who stopped asking permission.
