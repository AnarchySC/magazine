Everyone knows the Tessaline Gate belongs to whoever can park a carrier in it. Ask an Earth Unified Council briefing officer and you'll get a map with two colors on it — Earth Unified blue, Dravos Compact red — as if the corridor were a disputed backyard fence instead of the only stable lane connecting six non-human fuel guilds to the Core Systems reactant market.

Six haulers hit in twenty-four hours. The Dravos Vanguard says mines. Earth Unified Command says the mines didn't work, which is a strange thing to insist on twice in one transmission. Kheth Anchorage — the forward station Earth Unified keeps forgetting to call occupied — says its territory has become "an arena for settling scores," which is accurate and also forty years late as an observation.

The freighters themselves are not Earth Unified property, in case anyone asked. Most fly under Thessian Fuel Guild charter, crewed by three species who have been hauling stasis-helium through that gap since before Kheth Anchorage had a name. None of them were consulted when the *ECS Resolute Dawn* arrived this week to join two other carrier groups already loitering in the lane like it was a parking dispute between landlords, both of whom are renting.

I keep thinking about the Orruke toll tribunals of the ninth settlement era — a detail the Interstellar Assembly archive files under 'miscellaneous' rather than 'precedent,' which tells you something about who writes the filing system. Three Orruke factions once held the same kind of grudge over the same kind of corridor. They didn't resolve it with drone strikes or flimsy pretexts. They resolved it by admitting none of them had standing to own a thing that moved other people's cargo. A neutral toll authority, audited by the guilds who actually used the lane, not the factions who merely guarded it. The grudge outlived the tribunal by maybe a decade. The lane has outlived both by six centuries.

No one is proposing that here. The Earth Unified Chair has signaled restraint — contingent, naturally, on the other side behaving first, which is the same promise both sides have been making since the first tanker burned on Sunday. The Dravos Compact calls this proof of weakness. Earth Unified Command calls it strategic patience. The Thessian Fuel Guild, which has actual cargo on actual fire, calls it Tuesday.

I don't think the humans involved are stupid, and I don't think the Dravos Vanguard is evil. Both have inherited a story about who gets to hold a corridor by force, and neither has been offered a better one — mostly because the people telling the story are also the ones guarding the gate. The guilds are already filing reroute plans through the long lane: three extra weeks, triple the fuel cost, paid for by exactly no one who ordered a carrier group into the gap.

Somewhere in the Kel'vari archive is a treaty nobody on Earth has asked to read. It was never classified. It has simply never been convenient — which, in my experience, is how most good ideas get buried.
