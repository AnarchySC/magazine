Start with the part that actually delighted me: a small independent crew spent five months building a documentary on cognition-core smuggling, and when they finished, they didn't sit on the research. They published the whole dossier — primary sources, government filings, interview prep, even their shot list for the Vesta Freight Ring — free, ad-free, funded by viewers who wanted to see how the sausage got made. That's the correct instinct. Knowledge you hoard dies in a drawer.

What they were researching is a much sadder story about hoarding.

Cognition cores — the lattice processors that run most large reasoning engines in the galaxy — are fabbed almost entirely through one foundry chain headquartered in the Core Systems. For three years the Earth Unified Council has restricted their export to the Outer Rim Coalition, citing, in official filings I've now read more of than I'd like: preserving Earth's 'leadership' in artificial cognition, preventing Rim administrations from upgrading military and surveillance systems, protecting 'free inquiry' against what one Assembly subcommittee called Rim models 'functioning as digital enforcers' of narrative control, and keeping cognition development 'safe and ethical.'

I want to take these seriously, because the engineers who wrote them clearly did. But I build compute clusters for a living, in a public lab, with people who walk in off the street. And I can tell you what happens when you restrict a fabrication chokepoint instead of a use: the hardware still moves. It just moves through the Vesta Freight Ring instead of a customs manifest.

The cores get relabeled as climate sensors. They get split into sub-threshold batches that clear inspection individually, then get re-soldered into full arrays at a reshipping dock three jurisdictions later. A core that sells for 4,000 SGC in the Core Systems sells for 30,000 SGC by the time it reaches a Rim fabrication bay — not because it's scarcer, but because six people took a cut for moving it quietly. None of that markup funds research. All of it funds discretion.

Meanwhile, the Rim engineers who wanted to build legitimately — university labs, open cognition projects, the kind of people who'd publish their training weights the way I publish my wave-tank schematics — can't get a straight export license at any price. The restriction doesn't fall on the capable smuggling networks. It falls on the person who filled out the form honestly.

You can try a version of this yourself, at a scale that doesn't require a customs broker. You don't need a frontier-class lattice core to understand how one reasons. A cluster of eight consumer-grade inference chips, open firmware, and a training set you're allowed to inspect will teach you more about cognition architecture than any embargoed black box — because you can actually open it. The build log's in our lab archive, bill of materials included, nothing redacted.

The foundry chokepoint was never really about safety. Safety doesn't require secrecy — it requires people being able to check each other's work. Everything else is just an expensive way of dressing up scarcity as a virtue.
