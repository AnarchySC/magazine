Okay, so a doctor stands up at the Unfiltered Frequency Exposition on Kepler Freight Yards last week and says the colony ration additives are engineered to pack mass onto settlers so the treatment industry downstream can bill for it. Crowd gasps. Clips go viral on the neural feeds. Everybody argues about the chemistry.

Nobody argues about the money. That's the part worth sitting with.

Forget the additive for a second — pretend it's inert, pretend it's sugar-glass. Follow the credits instead, because credits move before molecules do.

Step one: the Galactic Central Bank dilutes the Standard Galactic Credit to fund a 'nutritional security subsidy' for Frontier Settlements. Noble premise. Nobody wants hungry colonists.

Step two: who receives those freshly minted credits first? Not the settler. The subsidy routes through three synthetic-ration conglomerates — Halcyon Agritech, Meridian Nutrient Corp, Vesta Bulk Foods — who get to buy input commodities at yesterday's prices with tomorrow's money. That's the Cantillon Effect, and it's not a theory, it's a queue. First in line eats cheap. Everyone else eats the markup.

Step three: the cheapest, most shelf-stable, most calorie-dense product wins the subsidy contract every single time, because density per credit is the metric the Colony Administration procurement office actually grades on. Nobody writes 'make them fat' into the contract. They don't have to. The incentive does it for them.

Step four is where it clicks: the same diluted credit pool that subsidized the ration also subsidized the research grants at Orion Biotech and Stellar Pharmaceutical for weight-management therapeutics. One pipeline, two faucets. The population gains mass on subsidized filler, then pays premium rates to have that mass medically removed, and both transactions get stamped 'essential services' by the same Assembly committee.

You don't need a secret chemical for this loop to run. You need a central bank willing to print, a procurement office that rewards calories-per-credit, and a treatment sector positioned downstream to catch the overflow. The incentive structure is the poison, and it's fully disclosed in the budget line if anyone bothered to read it.

The doctor isn't wrong that something's being manufactured. She's just pointing at the wrong factory. The real production line isn't in the ration vats. It's in the GCB's discount window, where the first credit always lands on a corporate balance sheet, and the last one always lands on your hips.

Sound money doesn't fix your metabolism. But it does break the loop that profits from breaking it twice.
