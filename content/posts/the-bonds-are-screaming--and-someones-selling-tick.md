Okay okay okay, let me draw this out for you—

Sovereign Duration Notes. SDNs. The boring-sounding paper that Colony Administrations issue when they want to spend money they don't have, promising to pay it back in Standard Galactic Credits over anywhere from six months to sixty years. This market is supposed to be the calmest room in the whole financial station. Pension funds sleep in it. Insurance guilds retire in it. It is NOT supposed to move like a Zero-G qualifier final.

And yet. Look at the long-duration paper out of the Core Systems right now — swings of 4, 5, even 7% in a single trading cycle on the Ceres Exchange. That's not a bond market. That's a dice table with a coupon attached.

Why now?

Step one: the Galactic Central Bank spent three cycles flooding every settlement from Earth to the Outer Rim Coalition with freshly minted credits — the thing they call "liquidity support," which is my polite industry term for "we printed until the presses smelled like smoke."

Step two: that new money doesn't land everywhere at once. It lands first at Stellar Financial, at Orion Trust, at the mega-corps with a direct feed to the GCB's discount window. They buy assets before the rest of the galaxy even knows the money exists. Prices go up. THEN the credit trickles down to colony payrolls and frontier settlements, where it just raises the price of fuel and protein paste.

That's the Cantillon Effect, baby! First in line eats steak. Last in line eats the receipt.

Step three — and this is the part that actually explains your screaming bonds — once everyone realizes the GCB can't keep printing without the credit losing value, they start demanding a premium to hold sixty-year paper. Nobody wants to lend the Earth Unified Council money for two generations at a fixed rate while the Cantillon conga line keeps dancing. So yields whipsaw. Duration becomes a casino chip. The "safe" asset stops being safe, because the thing backing it — the credit itself — stopped being stable first.

Enter the opportunists.

A neural-feed personality calling himself "The Insurgent Capitalist" has been running transmissions across three sectors this week, warning breathlessly that "bond volatility is skyrocketing" — true! — right before pivoting, every single broadcast, into a pitch for "Mutiny Capital Pro," his 40-credit-a-month signal relay that promises to show you "exactly how I'm profiting from the chaos."

Friends, I need you to see the shape of this. He is correctly diagnosing a fire he did not start, then selling you a subscription to his personal fire extinguisher. The actual cheat code isn't a signal relay. It's understanding that duration risk on a currency the GCB can dilute at will was never "safe" to begin with. You don't need Mutiny Capital Pro. You need to ask why a sixty-year promise from an institution that can rewrite the value of money overnight was ever priced like a sure thing.

That's not an alpha feed. That's just arithmetic. I'll send you the invoice.
