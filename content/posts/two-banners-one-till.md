Forty years back, hauling ice out past the Trojan camps, I ran routes against a man named Oduya. Different company flag on his hull, different prices painted on his manifest board, different sneer when our ships passed a relay beacon. Customers loved it. Competition, they figured, kept us honest.

It kept us rich. Oduya and I split the lanes before either of us undocked. Same source ice, same margin, two logos. The rivalry was the product. People will pay a premium to believe someone's fighting for them.

I think about that every time a scandal breaks involving two factions in the Interstellar Assembly who supposedly can't stand each other, who vote together on the budget lines nobody's watching and tear into each other on the neural feeds over the ones everybody is. It's not a conspiracy. It's a trade route with a better marketing department.

A data-cube's making the rounds on independent transmissions this cycle — pulled out of a derelict comm-buoy, recorded decades back by a relay broadcaster named Corwin Ashe. Ashe's pitch: every Colony Administration is captured by the same shadow council, politics is professional wrestling where the factions coordinate backstage, and the whole arrangement is following a script written into old scripture, cycling toward a scheduled unmaking. A frontier mystic running the Archaix Drift channel takes it further — a retrocausal Cognition-Core, AIX, already exists outside linear time and is steering the plot from the far future backward into now, same way it steered the Drowning of the Core Worlds a few thousand years before anyone built a ship.

My trouble with it isn't that it's false — I've got no way to prove a negative about shadow councils, and neither does anyone screaming at me across a feed. My trouble is it's gorgeous, and gorgeous theories do the same job gorgeous rivalries do: they give you a seat in the audience instead of a reason to check the manifest yourself.

You don't need a time-traveling machine-god to explain two Assembly delegates who hate each other on camera and dine together off it. You need a ledger. Oduya and I didn't require scripture. We required customers who assumed opposition proves independence.

Buried in Ashe's transmission, almost as an afterthought, is the one piece worth keeping: teach people how to think, not what to think. Give a kid the tools to trace an argument to its root instead of a finished conclusion to memorize. That's not mysticism. That's maintenance — the same discipline you'd use stripping a reactor down to find which valve is actually lying to the gauge.

The galaxy doesn't need a grand unified theory of its own captivity. It needs fewer people content to watch two hulls race past the same beacon and call it freedom. Check who profits when you cheer for one flag. Then check again when you switch to the other. If the number's the same, you've found your script — and it was never written in the stars. It was written on the invoice.
